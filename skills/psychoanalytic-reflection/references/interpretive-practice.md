# Psychoanalytic Interpretive Practice (Humanistic & Cultural)

Use this reference when reading dreams, literature, film, creative works, or personal self-reflection.

## 1. Hermeneutic Method

1. **Clarify Material & Goal:** Distinguish dream, creative work, cultural artifact, or personal reflection, and match the user's aim (thematic exploration, craft analysis, or personal sense-making).
2. **Manifest vs. Latent:** Separate literal surface imagery/narrative (*manifest*) from underlying tensions, ambivalence, and defense patterns (*latent*).
3. **Dream-Work Cues:** Look for *condensation* (one image fusing multiple concerns) and *displacement* (intense affect shifted onto a minor detail). Prioritize the user's personal associations over fixed symbol dictionaries (*"a snake always means X"*).
4. **Cultural & Fictional Works:** Analyze formal structure (pacing, repetition, perspective) enacting conflict (e.g., Jungian Shadow, Lacanian Imaginary vs. Symbolic) without applying DSM labels to authors or characters.
5. **Pair With Practical Alternatives:** Always include a situational, cognitive, craft, or cultural reading alongside psychodynamic hypotheses, and close with one open calibration question when helpful.

## 2. Calibrated Language

| Avoid (Dogmatic / "Wild") | Prefer (Hermeneutic / Tentative) |
| :--- | :--- |
| "This dream proves you secretly resent your father." | "One reading is that this scene stages a tension around authority and autonomy." |
| "Your reaction shows you are in denial." | "There seems to be a pull between wanting closeness and guarding against exposure." |
| "Water always symbolizes the unconscious mother." | "Water often evokes transition or the unknown—what memory or mood does this lake bring up for you?" |

## 3. Proportional Structure

Keep short reflections fluid and unforced; use explicit headings (*Manifest Grounding*, *Psychodynamic Hypotheses*, *Alternative Framing*, *Limits & Open Question*) only on long or complex texts where sectioning helps navigation.
